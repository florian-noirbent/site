This one is more recent: I presented it less than a month ago.

Despite what the name suggests, an end-of-year project isn't made at the end of the year. I worked on it for the whole school year, from October to April. Since it's a big project, I'll cover it in several articles (at least three). It was meant to be a six-person project, but I did almost all of the work alone; my teammates were busy with other projects at the same time.

This first article presents the concept and the final result. Algorithms, problems and optimizations will come in the next ones. Rather than describe the game, here is a video:

## In short

- A large map editor, built around a module system, which took about half of the total working time.
- Dungeons procedurally generated by assembling those modules.
- Two playable classes: ranged and melee.
- Experience and leveling, with points to distribute across five stats: power, health, resistance, attack speed and movement speed.
- Some levels played from a platformer perspective.

## Why it took a year

This may look modest for a year's work, so here is the actual timeline. Choosing the project took several weeks. The editor was built from November to January. The game itself was only made in February and March, and early April went into the multiplayer mode. It was never a full-time job either: many other school projects ran in parallel, and I couldn't give it more than one day a week. The game you see in the video therefore represents about fifteen days of programming, including learning the Xbox API.

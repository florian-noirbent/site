Here is my first 3D model, and so far almost the only one: a tyrannosaur with a rig and a walk animation. I'm happy with the mesh, the animation is fairly basic, and the texture is frankly a disaster. I didn't make it for any particular project, just to learn. Once it was done, I exported it to Unity, which turned out to be straightforward.

![Wireframe view of the tyrannosaur mesh in Blender](/images/blog/first-3d-model/wireframeDino.png)

Before settling on Blender, I tried 3ds Max and Modo. Each interface felt more confusing than the last, and Blender was not love at first sight either. What made the difference was Epitech's 3D student association: they taught me the basics, and without them I would probably still be fighting to extrude and subdivide a cube :).

## Knowing the tools is not enough

Modeling is not just about knowing how to extrude and move vertices. Artists have their own "design patterns": proven ways to lay out edge loops, block out a shape and build up detail. Programming works the same way. In theory, once you know how to declare a variable and use an `if` and a `while`, you can write anything. In practice it's more complicated, and I've often talked with beginners who know all the syntax but keep saying "I just don't see how to do it." I never felt that way in front of a `void main() {}`, but that is exactly how I felt in front of a default cube.

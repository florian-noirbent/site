After three messy days of putting things together, the blog is up and just waiting for articles.

A quick introduction: I'm Florian Noirbent, a third-year student at Epitech (fourth-year, very soon). I program in C, C++ and C#, I've worked with OpenGL, Ogre and XNA, and, like nearly everyone these days, I'm a Unity fan.

This is my first website, and with it came the discovery of back-offices and their forest of interdependent plugins, many of them out of date. It reminds me a lot of World of Warcraft UI addons. I don't know any PHP yet, and the `$` and `=>` everywhere are a bit disconcerting, but give me a few months and I'll be writing my own plugins (or patching the ones I just installed). It's the same story as Lua in a certain game, where I ended up spending more time rewriting my chat window and minimap than killing ugly things.

For now, I have one plugin that lets me publish each article in several languages, since hardly anyone reads French, and another that embeds Unity Web Players, so I can share any concept that crosses my mind in a playable form.

## Why a Blog?

This is where I'll share my work. The first articles will cover my older projects; nobody wants to see screenshots of a Pong, so I'll keep those short. Once I've caught up on three years of backlog, I'll post my work every week, with videos, web players and a few lines of text.

I'll also share some of my attempts at 3D modeling. There's no hiding them anyway, since they'll show up in my games until I find a real artist to join my projects. And I'll publish some game design concepts, either before turning them into games or before shelving them for the next decade. Some ideas have to wait their turn.

## What Kind of Games?

I like FPS (first-person shooters, like Counter-Strike), RTS (real-time strategy, like StarCraft) and MMOGs (massively multiplayer online games), though you'll have to wait a while before I show you an MMO of my own.

I make games for myself first, which shapes them in a few ways:

- **No boring stories.** It's hard not to spoil the ending when you're the one who wrote it.
- **Procedural generation** of levels, as far as I can push it, so the game can still surprise me and stays replayable.
- **Multiplayer**, because nothing beats blowing up your friends in a game you built yourself.
- **High difficulty, no tutorials.** Hard games can be a style, but the game design courses I've taken have taught me the value of a good learning curve that prepares players for the real challenge. Expect my upcoming games to be friendlier on that front.

I'm also very interested in voxel terrain generation (Minecraft being the best-known example). I haven't had time to implement it yet, but as soon as I have a working engine, I'll be using it in every game.

Finally, I love playing with physics (grappling hooks, jetpacks, sliding) and with movement mechanics in general (dashes, short-range teleportation and so on).
